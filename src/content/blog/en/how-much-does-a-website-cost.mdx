Running a small business or a growing startup and thinking about building a website?

The first question always comes up fast:

**How much does it actually cost?**

The short answer: somewhere between **$0 and $15,000**.

But that number alone doesn't tell you much.

Because behind those prices, you're not talking about the same kind of site, or the same outcome.

Here's a clear breakdown of what you're really paying for.

---

## The different ways to build a website

There are several paths today, and they don't play in the same league at all.

---

### 1. Doing it yourself (Wix, Squarespace)

**Budget: $0 to $30/month**

Tools like these let you put a site together fast, with no technical skills required.

It's simple, it's quick, and it's limited.

In practice:
- generic design
- middling performance
- SEO that's hard to work with
- difficult to stand out from competitors

It's a fine way to test an idea. Not a way to build a real online presence.

---

### 2. WordPress with a template

**Budget: $500 to $2,000**

Very common among lower-cost freelancers.

The process usually looks the same:
- buy a theme
- quickly adapt it to your business
- go live

The problem?

Your site ends up looking like dozens of others.

And on top of that:
- performance is often mediocre
- plugin overload
- ongoing maintenance required

It's a middle ground, rarely a lasting solution.

---

### 3. A specialized freelance developer

**Budget: $1,500 to $6,000**

This is where things move into genuinely professional territory.

A specialized freelancer will:
- actually understand your business
- tailor the site to your audience
- optimize performance
- design around conversion

You also get a real advantage: a single point of contact, responsive, who knows your market.

For most small businesses, this is where the best value tends to land.

---

### 4. A web agency

**Budget: $5,000 to $15,000+**

Agencies offer end-to-end support:
- strategy
- design
- development
- ongoing follow-up

But they also carry higher costs:
- a full team
- office overhead
- project management layers

The result: higher quotes, sometimes without a meaningful difference in the final product for a standard business website.

---

## What actually drives the price

Two $3,000 websites can be completely different products.

Here's what actually matters.

---

### Number of pages

A simple 5-page site has little in common with a fuller build:
- detailed service pages
- a blog
- a portfolio of past work
- SEO-oriented content

More content means more work, plain and simple.

---

### Design

Template, or fully custom?

That's often what separates:
- a site that's "fine"
- a site that builds trust instantly

And that difference shows up directly in your conversion rate.

---

### Features

Every feature adds work:
- advanced forms
- online booking
- dynamic galleries
- third-party integrations

Nothing is technically "free."

---

### SEO

A site built with SEO in mind from day one saves you from:
- relying on paid ads forever
- having to rebuild everything later

Structure, tags, content, speed — SEO starts the moment the site is built, not after.

---

### Performance

Google factors Core Web Vitals directly into rankings today.

A slow site:
- ranks worse
- loses visitors before they even see your offer

Performance isn't a bonus anymore. It's the baseline.

---

## What should you actually budget?

If you're running a business and want a site that's:
- professional
- fast
- well-positioned in search
- built to generate leads

a realistic budget sits between **$2,500 and $6,000**.

Below that range:
- generic result
- limited impact
- likely to need a rebuild soon

Above it:
- more complete deliverables
- or the heavier structure of an agency

---

## Why a good website makes a real difference

Whatever market you're in, competition online is real, and growing.

Your website is often the first real contact a prospective client has with your business.

A slow, confusing, poorly built site loses people instantly.

A fast, clean, convincing one works for you around the clock.

---

## A real example

A small business can absolutely start with a simple site.

But between a basic templated site and one actually designed to build trust and convert, the results aren't close.

In some cases, a well-built site multiplies inbound inquiries without increasing the marketing budget at all.

---

## Questions worth asking before you choose

Before working with a freelancer or an agency, ask:

- Who's actually building the site?
- Is it optimized for mobile from the start?
- What performance are they willing to guarantee?
- Do I own the site outright?
- How do changes work after delivery?

The answers will tell you a lot about how serious they are.

---

## Conclusion

A website isn't an expense.

It's a business tool.

The right choice isn't the cheapest option, or the most expensive one. It's the one that actually delivers results.
