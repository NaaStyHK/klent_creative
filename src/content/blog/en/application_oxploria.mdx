When you land in Barcelona or La Rochelle, what do you do?

You open Google Maps. You search "Barcelona attractions" or "La Rochelle guided tours." And there it is...

You find exactly what everyone else sees. Same museums. Same churches. Same queues.

The classic tourist guide is a bit like that. Efficient for checking boxes, empty for real discovery.

## The real problem with standard tourist guides

A paper guide, or even a classic app, is...

**Static.** You check a map, follow a list, done.

**Generic.** Same content for everyone, regardless of your actual interests.

**Shallow.** "Barcelona Cathedral, built in 1298, a must-see." And that's it.

**No context.** Why does it matter? What does it actually say about the city?

And above all... **you end up exploring like a tourist, not like someone who's genuinely discovering.**

## Why a cultural geolocation app changes everything

Picture a **cultural geolocation app** that detects exactly where you are.

And tells you: "Two streets from here is a forgotten 19th-century sculpture. Here's the story of the sculptor, why it matters, and what it says about the era."

Not necessarily a famous landmark. But authentic. Curious. Intriguing.

That's different. It changes your relationship with the city.

## Oxploria: the new interactive guide for two cities

Oxploria is exactly that: a **cultural discovery app** built for authentic exploration.

It currently runs in two cities: **Barcelona and La Rochelle.**

### How it works (technically)

The app detects your location. It automatically surfaces cultural spots near you: modernist façades, sculptures, hidden passageways, historic buildings.

Or you pick your city manually and explore at your own pace.

Every spot comes with a **real description**: history, architectural context, why it matters, how it fits into the city's story.

### What a geolocation app like this actually changes

**You stop following a pre-packaged itinerary.**

Instead of "reach landmark 3, take the photo, move to attraction 4," you wander. You discover. The app only steps in if you want to know more.

**You find places other tourists skip.**

Not because they're less beautiful. Because classic guides simply don't mention them.

**Real history becomes accessible.**

Not 50 generic words. Rich context, written for mobile reading, that makes you want to dig further.

## Real examples in Barcelona

You're in **Gràcia** (touristy, but authentic).

With a paper guide: "A residential area with cute cafés."

With Oxploria: you discover the Art Nouveau façade on Carrer del Sol, dating back to 1910, why it matters architecturally, and what that style says about the era when Barcelona was culturally booming.

That's not a footnote. It changes how you see what's in front of you.

You walk through **El Born** and stumble on a forgotten 19th-century sculpture. The app tells you who made it, what it represents, and how it connects to old Barcelona.

You hadn't noticed the sculpture before. Now you know why it's there.

## Real examples in La Rochelle

La Rochelle isn't just the Vieux-Port and its three towers.

With a classic guide: you'll hit the obvious La Rochelle attractions. Then that's it.

With Oxploria: you discover the maritime architecture of the Vieux-Port's back streets, the façades that tell the story of maritime trade, the ornamental details nobody looks at.

An **interactive guided tour**, minus the guide. Just your phone and your curiosity.

The app also points you to hidden corners, not secret, just overlooked by standard tourist lists.
