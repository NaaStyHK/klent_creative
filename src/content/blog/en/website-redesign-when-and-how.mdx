A website doesn't age overnight. It ages gradually, until one day someone looks at it and thinks: *this isn't us anymore.*

The question then is whether to tweak it or rebuild it.

Here are the clear signals, the realistic budget, and how to do it without throwing away what already works.

---

## The signs it's time for a redesign

### The site no longer matches the business

You've changed positioning, services or the kind of client you serve, and the site is still telling the story from five years ago.

This is the most common reason. And the most expensive one to leave alone: every visitor gets a message that isn't yours anymore.

### It's slow

If it takes more than 3 seconds to load, you're losing visitors before they read anything.

Google also factors speed into rankings. A slow site loses twice.

### Mobile is painful

Over 60% of traffic comes from a phone. If people have to pinch to zoom, if buttons are tiny, or if anything overflows, the problem is serious.

"It displays on mobile" isn't the same as being built for mobile.

### It doesn't generate enquiries

You get visits but no messages. That's almost always a structure and clarity problem, not a traffic problem.

### It's become hard to update

If every small change means calling someone, the site has become a brake rather than a tool.

---

## Tweak or rebuild: how to decide

**A refresh is enough** when the technical foundation is solid and you only need to update copy, images or a few sections.

**A full redesign makes sense** when:

- the technology is outdated or poorly maintained
- the structure can't accommodate growth
- performance is bad at the root
- the brand has genuinely changed

Rule of thumb: if you're going to touch more than half the site, rebuilding usually costs less than patching.

---

## Budget to plan for

**Visual refresh** (same foundation, new design): $1,000 to $2,500

**Full redesign** (new structure, new design, new technical base): $2,500 to $8,000

**Redesign with functionality** (e-commerce, booking, multilingual, integrations): $6,000 to $15,000+

Below $1,500, you're typically getting a lightly adapted template. It can get you by, but it rarely fixes the underlying problems.

---

## What you must not lose in a rebuild

This is the critical part, and where most redesigns go wrong.

### Your search rankings

If you change URLs without redirects, you lose the ranking you'd built up. Sometimes years of it.

**Every old URL must 301-redirect to its new equivalent.** No exceptions.

### The content that performs

Before touching anything, look at which pages bring traffic and enquiries. Those get kept and improved, not deleted.

A redesign that removes your best-ranking pages is a redesign that destroys value.

### Your data

Export a history of your analytics before the switch. Without a baseline, you won't know whether the redesign worked.

---

## What a well-run redesign looks like

1. **Audit** — what works, what doesn't, which pages rank
2. **Strategy** — goals, audience, structure, messaging
3. **Design** — wireframes, identity, experience
4. **Development** — build, performance, technical SEO
5. **Migration** — redirects, checks, go-live
6. **Follow-up** — measurement and adjustments over the following weeks

Typical timeline: 4 to 10 weeks depending on scope.

If someone proposes skipping the audit and the redirects, treat it as a red flag.

---

## Conclusion

A redesign isn't an aesthetic whim. It's a business decision.

The question isn't "does our site look dated?" — it's "what is it costing us every month as it stands?"

And doing it properly means improving without throwing away what was already working.
