More and more companies are considering launching a mobile app.

And the first question is always the same: **how much does it cost?**

The short answer: somewhere between $3,000 and $60,000. A range so wide it's useless on its own.

Here's what actually sits behind those numbers.

---

## Do you really need an app?

Before the budget, this question.

An app makes sense when:

- your users come back frequently
- you need push notifications
- you use device features (camera, GPS, sensors)
- you need it to work offline

If your need is mostly informational or one-off contact, a well-built website does more for considerably less.

An app isn't a shop window. It's a tool for repeated use.

---

## App types and what they cost

### Native app

Built separately for iOS and Android, in each platform's own language.

Maximum performance and full device access. Also twice the work, and therefore twice the budget.

Powerful, but expensive.

### Cross-platform app

One codebase for both platforms, using Flutter or React Native.

This is the most common choice in 2026: performance very close to native, at meaningfully lower cost and shorter timelines.

### No-code app

Built on a visual platform. Fast and cheap to start, limited as soon as the project grows.

Worth considering for an MVP, rarely viable long term.

---

## Budgets by complexity

**Simple app** — catalogue, content, forms, no complex user accounts
**$3,000 to $8,000**

**Mid-range app** — user accounts, database, notifications, admin panel
**$8,000 to $20,000**

**Complex app** — payments, messaging, advanced geolocation, external system integrations
**$20,000 to $60,000+**

These figures reflect an experienced professional. A "fast and cheap" app is almost always a warning sign: somebody is cutting somewhere, and it usually gets paid for later.

---

## Realistic timelines

- Simple app: 1 to 2 months
- Mid-range app: 2 to 4 months
- Complex app: 4 to 8 months or more

On top of that comes store submission: Apple's review can take from a few days to a couple of weeks, and sometimes comes back requiring changes.

A developer promising a complex app in three weeks is promising you a problem.

---

## What almost nobody mentions: maintenance

An app isn't delivered and forgotten.

iOS and Android ship new versions every year. Without updates, an app stops working properly before long.

Budget **10–20% of the initial cost per year** for maintenance, fixes and iteration.

If a quote doesn't mention maintenance, an important part of the conversation is missing.

---

## Start small, grow later

The most expensive mistake is trying to launch everything at once.

Ship a first version with the essentials, put it in front of real users, and let actual usage tell you what to build next.

It's cheaper, faster and far less risky than spending six months on features nobody may ever open.

---

## Questions to ask before signing

- Who owns the code once the project is delivered?
- What technology are you using, and why that one?
- What exactly does maintenance cover?
- Who publishes the app to the stores, and under which accounts?
- Have you built this kind of app before?

The answers tell you a lot about who you're dealing with.

---

## Conclusion

A mobile app isn't a branding expense. It's a tool that has to solve something concrete.

For projects under $20,000, a specialised professional usually offers the best balance of cost, quality and responsiveness.

And the point isn't to launch an app. It's to launch an app your users actually use.
